Summa privacy policy

Last updated: 21 November 2026

Summa is made by Briqvo (Abdullah Ventures LLC). This policy explains what happens to your information when you use the Summa app for Android and iPhone.

The short version

  • Your library (notes, flashcards, quizzes, chats, scores and review schedule) stays on your phone.
  • When you make notes, the material you add is sent to our server and from there to the AI services that read it (listed below), including speech-to-text for recordings. Our server holds your upload only in memory while it's read.
  • The finished notes are kept on our server for 3 days so they can be fetched again if your connection drops, then deleted.
  • Flashcards you share are published at a public link until you delete them (or for a year).
  • There's no sign-up. Summa doesn't ask for your name, email or phone number. No ads.

On your phone only

Everything in your library (notes, flashcards, quiz questions and scores, your chat with your notes, and when each card is due) is kept in the app's private storage on your phone. Recordings are deleted after they're sent. Uninstalling Summa deletes all of it. We have no copy, so we can't recover it for you.

Making notes

When you add a source, Summa sends it to our server: the recording, file (PDF, PowerPoint or Word), photos, or text you pasted, plus an optional name. Nothing else from your library goes with it.

  • Our server (on Cloudflare) holds the upload only in memory while it's processed (usually under two minutes). It is never written to storage, logs or backups. The result (title, summary, notes, flashcards, quiz and, for recordings, the transcript) is saved for 3 days, tied to your random account ID, so the app can fetch it after a dropped connection; then it's deleted automatically.
  • Cloudflare Workers AI runs the AI for everyone, including free trials, unless a provider below is used for a paying subscriber. These are open AI models hosted by Cloudflare on its own network. It receives the material (recordings, PDFs, slides, photos or text) and our instructions and sends back the result. Cloudflare states that it doesn't use this content to train AI models. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.
  • For paying subscribers, we may instead use: Anthropic (Claude), which receives the material and our instructions and writes the notes, flashcards and quiz. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, data sent through its API is not used to train its models; Anthropic may keep requests for a limited time for safety and legal reasons. See Anthropic's privacy policy.
  • For paying subscribers, we may instead use: Groq (speech-to-text), which receives recordings only, and returns the words. See Groq's privacy policy.

Record only lectures and talks you're allowed to record, and don't add material about other people that they wouldn't expect you to share.

Chat with your notes

When you ask a question, the app sends that source's notes, the question and the last few messages of the chat to our server and on to our AI provider. Nothing is stored on our server. The chat itself is kept on your phone.

YouTube

Summa doesn't download anything from YouTube. If you paste a video's transcript, it's handled like any other pasted text.

Shared flashcards

When you tap "Share flashcards", that source's title, subject and flashcards (only those) are published on a public web page so anyone with the link can study them. The page isn't listed in search engines. You can unpublish it by deleting the source in the app; otherwise it expires after a year. Anyone can report a deck to us, and we remove decks that break the law or our terms.

Invites

Your invite code is linked to your random account ID. When a friend uses it, our server records that their account came from yours and whether they've made their first notes, so the invite reward can be given. We never learn who your friends are beyond their random account IDs. On Android, the app reads the Play Store install referrer once, only to see whether it was installed from a shared deck or an invite link.

Your Summa account

Summa has no sign-up. Our server creates a random account ID the first time the app connects, and keeps for it: when it was made, how many sources, recording minutes and chat questions you've used in the last week (for the free plan and fair-use limits), whether your plan is active, your invite code and invites. The ID is stored on your phone.

Cloudflare handles network information such as IP addresses to deliver requests and stop attacks, and our server keeps short-lived error logs that never contain your material.

Plans and purchases

Plans are bought through Google Play or the App Store; we never see your payment details. RevenueCat, our subscription service, receives your account ID and your purchase history for Summa, and our server asks RevenueCat whether your plan is active. See RevenueCat's privacy policy.

If you start a free trial, Summa sets a reminder on your phone for 2 days before you're charged. It's a local notification: no push service is involved.

What we don't do

  • We don't sell your information or share it for advertising. Summa has no ads.
  • We don't keep your recordings, files or photos on our server.
  • We don't use your material for anything except making your notes.
  • Summa has no analytics of its own at this time. If that changes, this policy will say so before it ships.

Children

Summa is for students aged 13 and over. It isn't directed at children under 13.

Your choices and rights

You can delete any source in the app at any time (which also unpublishes its shared deck), or everything by uninstalling. To delete what our server keeps for your account ID (usage counts, invite records) or purchase records held by RevenueCat, contact us at briqvo@gmail.com.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date before the change takes effect.
